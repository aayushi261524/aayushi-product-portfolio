01 Fintech · Merchant decisions

RayPayment Strategy Agent

Helping merchants understand commercial terms, compare permitted payment routes, and approve an exact change.

A successful payment can still take the wrong commercial route.

Agentic experienceDecision supportHuman approval
Inside the prototype / Route comparisonActual product screenshot · click to enlarge ↗
Try Ray ↗View source ↗

Independent concept · fictional data · simulated payments integration

Explore the product thinkingProblem, journey, decisions & prototype boundaries

The problem behind the interface

For merchants managing payment providers, bank offers, and commercial agreements. Routing decisions need to reflect offer eligibility, provider commitments, fees, and completion assumptions. Comparing one percentage in isolation can miss the wider commercial constraint.

The product idea

Bring interpretation, simulation, and approval into one guided workspace. Ray turns a merchant’s goal and confirmed terms into editable decisions, checks a candidate route, then shows the exact before-and-after change.

The value being explored is a more informed, controlled routing decision. Financial benefits remain estimates until outcome evidence exists.

From intent to a checked change

  1. 01Choose a goal: completion, costs, agreements, or a preferred split.
  2. 02Add terms and clarify scope, eligibility, and backup permissions.
  3. 03Compare permitted alternatives using explicit economic inputs.
  4. 04Review exact fields, baseline revision, constraints, and estimated impact.
  5. 05Approve a simulated change and inspect configuration read-back.

Three decisions visible in the build

Keep evidence beside the conversation.

The evidence panel exposes terms, assumptions, and calculations while the merchant makes a decision. The proposal has an inspectable basis.

Separate a suggestion from permission.

Normal allocation and fallback authorisation are separate choices. Infeasible constraints or missing readiness stop the change.

Bind approval to an exact scope.

A proposal carries the saved configuration revision. Concurrent edits and read-back mismatches trigger safe stops; unrelated rules are preserved.

Working in the prototype

Create and review journeys; local term parsing; deterministic checks and comparisons; editable decisions; approval, rejection, and failure scenarios.

Simulated / not connected

Scripted conversation, fictional merchants and providers, local configuration writes and read-back. No live model, Optimizer connection, real payments, or verified financial recovery. Undo is planned.

What I would validate next

Test whether merchants can identify scope and assumptions before approving. Validate contract interpretation, real provider capability mapping, and reconciliation against actual outcomes.

Grounded in the repository, its architecture, and the live journey. Independent concept; not an official Razorpay release.

HormonyFrom body signals to a next step

A wearable-and-app concept exploring how women could understand changing signals in the context of sleep, symptoms, and daily life.

More signals are useful only when they help someone understand their day.

The problem behind the interface

For women trying to make sense of changes in energy, sleep, stress, and cycle-related experiences. A reading alone leaves the next question unanswered: what might this mean for my day?

Ananya is the prototype’s fictional persona, used to make that journey tangible.

The product idea

Explore a connected experience that compares signals with a personal baseline, adds context through a brief check-in, explains a possible pattern, and suggests a manageable next step.

The prototype makes the proposed service concrete across 18 guided steps, from onboarding to a flexible weekly plan.

From a signal to everyday guidance

  1. 01Understand the promise, share goals, and choose a support plan.
  2. 02Follow the simulated band setup and seven-day baseline story.
  3. 03See a personal profile and add context with a quick check-in.
  4. 04Explore an explanation, one next step, alerts, and Ask Sakhi.
  5. 05Explore Plus planning, a simulated coach appointment, and longer-term patterns.

Three decisions visible in the build

Explain before recommending.

“What changed”, “what else happened”, and “what it may mean” precede the next step. The structure makes the reasoning visible without presenting an association as certainty.

Ask for context at the useful moment.

A short check-in sits within the insight journey. It explores how to add relevant context with less logging effort.

Make deeper support contextual.

Core focuses on understanding and a next step. Plus introduces day and week planning and coach access when the story reaches a planning need.

Working in the prototype

Clickable onboarding, symptom and plan selection, check-ins, an 18-step story, example explanations, and selectable coach slots. Visitors can move directly between journey steps.

Simulated / not connected

Band pairing, hormone readings, trends, Sakhi responses, recommendations, subscriptions, and coaching are demonstrations. No sensor connection or clinical validation is evidenced. Wellness concept, not a diagnostic product.

What I would validate next

Test whether users understand the explanations and uncertainty. Validate sensing feasibility and accuracy, privacy and consent, and whether suggested actions feel useful and manageable.

Grounded in the live app journey and its local implementation. Related Hormony pages are one concept, rather than separate projects. No public GitHub repository verified.

03 Travel · Connected journeys

MyraTravel Autopilot

A travel companion concept connecting trip preferences, planning, and in-trip assistance when circumstances change.

A change to one activity can affect the whole trip.

Case-competition concept · simulated bookings and responses

Explore the product thinkingProblem, journey, decisions & prototype boundaries

The problem behind the interface

For travellers coordinating preferences, bookings, and changes across a trip. When an activity is disrupted, choosing a replacement can also affect transfers, hotel nights, costs, and companions’ comfort.

The family demo makes this concrete with a trip planned around parents, a budget, food preferences, and a relaxed pace.

The product idea

Carry the trip context from the first request into planning and in-trip support. Myra presents an interpreted brief for confirmation, offers destination choices, and later compares recovery plans with their wider consequences.

The intended benefit is less context to re-explain and a clearer change to approve.

From a trip brief to a recovery choice

  1. 01Describe the trip and confirm the interpreted preferences.
  2. 02Compare destinations and preview a selected itinerary.
  3. 03Review and approve a fictional booking.
  4. 04Explore the impact of a simulated weather disruption.
  5. 05Compare recovery costs, timing, and booking changes before selecting an option.

Three decisions visible in the build

Confirm the brief before planning.

The interpreted intent card exposes duration, companions, budget, food, visa preference, and pace. Travellers can inspect the assumptions before choosing a destination.

Model the trip as connected choices.

The disruption view includes the activity, hotel, transfer, day plan, budget, and companions. Recovery options show consequences beyond the original activity.

Compare trade-offs before action.

A side-by-side view makes extra cost, refund impact, timing, walking, and changes required visible. Proposed actions remain subject to traveller approval.

Working in the prototype

A guided family planning and recovery flow, destination choices, intent confirmation, itinerary preview, simulated booking approval, and recovery comparison. The product also offers solo and group sample journeys.

Simulated / not connected

Sample trips, responses, bookings, payments, weather, and supplier interactions. Example prices and travel conditions are demonstration data. This is a case-competition concept, not evidence of a live MakeMyTrip integration.

What I would validate next

Test whether travellers understand linked impacts and approval scope. Validate supplier availability, booking-change reliability, preference retention, and safeguards for partial failures.

Grounded in the live product and a walkthrough of the family journey from intent through recovery comparison. No public source repository verified.

04 Strategy · Interactive modelling

NexBridgeStrategy Navigator

A case-based decision lab for comparing seven ways to own, operate, and fund a bank-to-ERP connectivity product.

A recommendation is stronger when you can show what would change it.

Scenario explorationEvidence confidenceDecision modelling
Inside the prototype / Sensitivity explorationActual product screenshot · click to enlarge ↗
Try NexBridge ↗Decision audit ↗

Case-based exploration · illustrative scores and scenarios

Explore the product thinkingProblem, journey, decisions & prototype boundaries

The problem behind the interface

For a strategy team evaluating a product inside a services business. The NexBridge case asks how Arden Digital should scale a bank-to-ERP connectivity product while managing investment, customer concentration, services relationships, and control.

A single recommendation can hide which judgements and evidence gaps drive the answer.

The product idea

Turn the strategy argument into an explorable decision tool. A ten-scene Model Demo connects options, criteria, financial hurdles, funding gates, sensitivity, and robustness. A Deep Dive exposes the underlying evidence and calculations.

The intended benefit is an inspectable recommendation: a reviewer can see the assumptions, challenge them, and understand how the conclusion moves.

From options to a conditional recommendation

  1. 01Explore seven ownership and operating-model options.
  2. 02Inspect twelve criteria and confidence-adjusted scoring.
  3. 03Review financial hurdles, staged funding, and fallback paths.
  4. 04Change capital, control, or partner-evidence assumptions.
  5. 05Explore robustness and return to the evidence in Deep Dive.

Three decisions visible in the build

Show the confidence behind a score.

The model adjusts fit ratings for evidence confidence. A weakly evidenced option does not silently receive the same certainty as a better-supported one.

Make the conclusion challengeable.

Sensitivity sliders and scenario presets recalculate the rankings. Improving the illustrative partner evidence can change the leading option.

Connect strategy to staged action.

Funding gates and fallback paths make the recommendation conditional on proof. The Deep Dive separates case facts, derived calculations, and assumptions.

Working in the prototype

Ten-scene navigation; option explanations; sensitivity controls and scenario presets; robustness exploration; twelve decision-question views; a financial model and final comparison.

Modelled / not observed

Case-based evidence, analyst-assigned scores and confidence factors, hypothetical funding gates, and scenario tests. Rankings and simulations are model outputs, not proven strategy performance or live company data.

What I would validate next

Test whether reviewers can explain why the leader changes. Review weights and ratings with decision-makers; replace evidence gaps with verified terms; calibrate adoption and cost assumptions before capital decisions.

Grounded in the live Vercel product, its local implementation, and the connected decision-audit Site. Presented as a case-based decision tool; no public GitHub repository verified.

When waiting has a cost, would someone pay for a faster human connection?

Joint submission · interactive concept · simulated calls and payments

Explore the proposalAudience, service journey, pricing & validation plan

Who needs a faster connection?

A caller with an urgent issue that automated support has not resolved. The submission uses fictional situations such as a damaged order near its return deadline, broadband failing before a work deadline, and a debited transfer awaiting resolution.

The opportunity being explored is the cost of waiting during a specific event, rather than a continuous need for a subscription.

The proposed service

Offer a choice between continuing with standard free support and purchasing priority routing to an available human agent at a verified business. The business would retain its existing support system and control eligibility and priority capacity.

The proposal sells priority routing, not a guaranteed resolution. It depends on enterprise partnerships and integration; the submitted screens illustrate the idea.

The illustrated journey

  1. 01Start a business support call and see the proposed AI-answered disclosure.
  2. 02Compare the free route with an optional paid human-priority offer.
  3. 03Review the one-time charge and use the illustrated UPI payment flow.
  4. 04Return to the call after the payment confirmation.
  5. 05Connect through the proposed priority lane, subject to partner capacity.

Product choices in the submission

Charge for an occasional event.

The recommended launch hypothesis is ₹19 once, with ₹9–₹49 to be tested across different stakes. The visual retains the earlier ₹29 hypothesis. These are proposed prices, not evidence of willingness to pay.

Keep the free support route available.

The proposal keeps standard AI and human support free, presents wait times as estimates, and limits priority slots through business-controlled capacity. The fairness risk to the standard queue still needs testing.

Compare alternatives before selecting a model.

The submission weighs the priority lane against enriched caller context and a lender-focused recovery tool. It selects the event-based consumer model for its proposed urgency trigger and fit with a one-time purchase.

What the submission contains

The original two-page proposal and a new clickable browser demo. Try fictional scenarios, free support, optional priority, payment success or failure, capacity limits, and cancellation with a simulated refund. Submission jointly authored by Aayushi and Saurav Pampattiwar.

Not built or validated here

The browser demo uses scripted responses, fictional businesses, and simulated payments, refunds, waits, and human connections. No real AI-call detection, business verification, payment or routing integration, measured conversion, or realised revenue. No affiliation with Truecaller is implied.

What to validate next

Test offer timing, willingness to pay, partner capacity, and connection reliability. Measure incremental revenue per eligible support event, conversion and servicing costs, alongside complaints and standard-queue abandonment.

Based on the original TrueValue submission. Revenue funnel rates and purchase behaviour are planning assumptions. The proposed pilot should resolve whether the offer appears at AI disclosure or after a confirmed request for human support.

Patterns across my projects

The interface is only part of the answer.

A useful recommendation
needs context,
consequences, and control.

Ray, Hormony, Myra, NexBridge, and Priority Human Connect address different situations. The connection is how they organise a choice: establish the relevant context, show what an option changes, and let the person inspect the recommendation.

01

Ask for the missing context.

Hormony places a check-in beside the explanation. Myra confirms companions, pace, and preferences before planning. Both explore how a small amount of context can make guidance more relevant.

02

Show what the choice changes.

Ray puts commercial assumptions beside the routing comparison. NexBridge lets a reviewer adjust model inputs. Each exposes the basis of the answer rather than leaving it inside a recommendation.

03

Keep consequential actions inspectable.

Ray separates a proposal from approval. Myra compares recovery plans before a booking change. These flows explore how to give users control when a choice affects money or existing plans.
